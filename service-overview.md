## The problem

A bad senior engineering hire doesn't just cost a salary — it costs months of runway, team momentum, and trust. It usually happens the same way: an interview process that's inconsistent, under-documented, and leans on gut feel more than evidence. By the time it's clear something's wrong, it's expensive to fix.

## What I do

I lead the interview process for engineering hires — end to end. I'm not a recruiter: I don't source or screen candidates, and I don't work on volume. I design the interview loop, run structured interviews, and deliver an evidence-based hire/no-hire report your team can actually stand behind.

## How it works

**1. Kickoff**
A working session with you and your team to define exactly what "great" looks like for this role — not a generic job spec, the real bar.

**2. Loop design**
A structured interview plan, mapped to the competencies that actually matter for this hire, agreed with you before a single interview happens.

**3. Structured interviews**
Every stage scored against a consistent rubric, with evidence captured in the room — not reconstructed from memory afterward.

**4. Evidence-based report**
A clear recommendation, with the reasoning laid out competency by competency, including the risks if you hire and my honest confidence level.

## Why it's different

- Every score is backed by specific evidence, not impressions.
- I work with your team, not around them — understanding what "great" looks like comes from the people who'll manage and depend on this hire.
- I'll never recommend a hire I don't believe is right, even when it's not the easier answer.
- Fees are bespoke to the role and the effort required — I charge per hire, not per interview, because what you're paying for is the outcome.

## Who this is for

Engineering leaders hiring senior or high-stakes roles who want a rigorous, structured process without building and running it themselves — and who'd rather hear an honest no than a convenient yes.
